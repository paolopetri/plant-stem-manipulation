## Decisions
- Damping is expressed as one time constant `damping_time` τ [s] in `stem.yaml` (stiffness-proportional: bend τ·E·I, stretch τ·E·A, twist τ·G·J), computed in `stem_cfg()`. Scales with the diameter like the stiffness; damping ratio of a mode is ζ = τ·ω/2. Value stays null until the stem stands in `check_stem.py` (τ ≈ 3.2 ms would give ζ = 0.05 for the first bending mode). Later the same day: set to 3.2 ms for the read-back check.
- `twist_modulus: null` for now: Newton then sets twist stiffness = bend stiffness (GJ = EI, i.e. Poisson ratio 0).
- Floats with an exponent in the yaml need a signed exponent (`5.0e+8`): PyYAML reads `5.0e8` as a string.
- Axial strain is measured as the gap at the joint along the tangent of segment i, not as the centre distance (which reports about -1.25e-3 false compression at the curvature limit, vs. 4e-5 real strain per 1 N).
- Stretch damage limit is a tensile stress (`damage.max_tensile_stress` [Pa], replaces `max_axial_strain`): stress = E · strain, tension only. A material property, so it stays fixed under stiffness/diameter randomization. Enforced in stage 1 once a threshold is chosen (changes yesterday's "bending limit only").
- Stem base: kinematic root segment instead of pins (full clamp including twist, removes a body from the solve instead of adding stiff joints; flexible length 0.38 m).
- Stem stiffness and solver: adopt the softened stretch/shear setting for now (user decision) and raise it with the deformables expert (docs/TODO.md, open questions). Rescaling cannot make the physical values converge: the deciding quantity is a geometric ratio.
- `point_pose` offset is measured from the segment centre along the tangent [m].

## Proposed stem solver setting and its trade-offs (adopted the same day, see Decisions)
Proposal from the sweep above: `stretch_modulus` 5e7 Pa (0.1 x bend modulus), new `shear_modulus` 5e5 Pa (0.001 x bend modulus), damping on bend and twist only, 8 substeps x 10 VBD iterations at a 10 ms step.

Why not the physical values: a real stem is far stiffer against stretching and shearing than against bending. The VBD solver works iteratively and cannot resolve that stiffness ratio at an affordable cost: with stretch = shear = bend modulus and 8 x 20 iterations the stem is about 23 times too soft in bending and falls over. Bending is what the task and the curvature limit depend on, so bending accuracy gets priority and the other two directions are softened.

What we give up:
- Stretching: the stem is 10 times softer along its axis than the bend modulus implies. 1 N of pull stretches the 0.4 m stem by 0.16 mm (EA / L = 6283 N/m). The tensile-stress limit still works, because stress = stretch modulus actually used x strain = force / area. Full stretch stiffness is not possible: the kick test blows up.
- Shearing: a real thin stem has practically no shear deformation. With the soft shear spring, shear adds about 10 % to the deflection (1.49 mm of 15.66 mm self-weight sag; about 8 % for a force at the tip). So the simulated stem is about 10 % softer in bending than EI alone says, and the cantilever check must compare against bending + shear, not against q L^4 / (8 E I) alone. Making shear softer still (0.0003 x) converges with even fewer iterations but raises this share to 35 %.
- Damping: stretch and shear vibrations are not damped by the material model, only by the solver's numerical damping (ratio about 0.01-0.02). Damping them (tau x E A) slows the solver's convergence and makes settings below cost 160 drift or blow up.
- Speed vs. accuracy: cost 80 (8 x 10) has about 1 % static error and takes 8.6 ms per 10 ms step with 1024 envs; cost 160 (8 x 20) has about 0.2 % error and takes 16.8 ms. Cost 40 is not usable (the stem does not return upright).

What we keep: static bending within about 1 % of the converged model, first bending frequency about 5 Hz, damping ratio 0.05-0.06 as set by `damping_time`, the stem returns upright after a kick.

Limits of this result: measured with the placeholder stem (0.4 m, 8 mm, 20 segments, E = 5e8 Pa), one env, no contact and no robot. The usable ratios depend on segment length / diameter (the locking grows with (segment length / diameter)^2) and must be re-checked when the stem parameters change, when they are randomized, and in the coupled scene with the fork.
